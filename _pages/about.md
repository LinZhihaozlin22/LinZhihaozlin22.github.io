I am an AI Systems Research Assistant at the University of Virginia, working on large language models, adaptive generation, representation learning, and AI systems. My current work profiles vLLM-based agentic workloads to identify concurrency and inference bottlenecks, with a focus on KV-cache management and serving efficiency. More broadly, my research studies how language models can adapt their generation to the input at inference time, and what their internal representations make possible in that regard.

Before joining UVA, I did independent LLM research. Together with Muyao Kong, I co-developed Scenario-Actor, a scenario-adaptive decoding framework that learns scenario representations from Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct hidden states and uses them to select a sampling temperature for each prompt. Our paper, "Read the Room Before Your Model Generates," was accepted to the AACL-IJCNLP 2026 Main Conference.

Before that, I spent over three years as a Software Development Engineer at Amazon Web Services, working on Amazon Connect. I built and operated metrics services handling 280K TPS as daily customer interactions grew from 10M+ to 20M+, owned a metrics validation service, and built retrieval-grounded, tool-integrated LLM workflows in production. That experience shapes how I approach research. I look for methods that hold up at scale, and for evaluation that is honest about what it does and does not show.

I received an M.S. in Computer Science from Santa Clara University and a B.S. in Computer Science, *cum laude*, from the University at Buffalo.
