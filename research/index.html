Featured work

Read the Room Before Your Model Generates

Muyao Kong and Zhihao Lin — authors contributed equally. Under review at EMNLP 2026. Paper (PDF)

Sampling temperature is usually treated as a fixed global knob, or calibrated at the token level. Scenario-Actor asks a different question: can the decoding temperature be derived from a geometrically interpretable representation of the communicative setting a prompt implies — an academic discussion, a casual exchange, an adversarial probe?

The framework has two parts. Scenarize reads the hidden-state matrix at a chosen Transformer layer and produces a scenario embedding, a 32-dimensional representation learned from 4096-dimensional Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct hidden states with a triplet loss over prompt triplets. Actout then maps that embedding to a prompt-level temperature, using interpretable scenario axes built from opposing subscenario vectors and grounded in the DIAMONDS model of situation characteristics from social psychology.

The manuscript evaluates the approach on GSM8K, AlpacaEval, and MT-Bench against fixed-temperature, greedy, random, nucleus sampling, and EDT baselines. On AlpacaEval, Scenario-Actor reaches 30.03% length-controlled win rate, the strongest result among the decoding methods evaluated. The analysis also covers the geometry of the learned scenario space via PCA and UMAP, and reports agreement between LLM-based evaluators and human annotators.

Research themes

Adaptive LLM generation

The area my completed work sits in. Rather than treating inference-time hyperparameters as fixed, I am interested in deriving them from structure the model already computes.

  • Scenario-aware and context-conditioned decoding
  • Inference-time adaptation
  • What Transformer hidden states encode about communicative context, and how to make those representations interpretable rather than merely predictive

LLM and AI systems

Where my engineering background and research interests meet. Research methods are only useful if they hold up under real workloads and honest measurement.

  • Retrieval-grounded systems
  • Tool-integrated LLM workflows and structured outputs
  • Evaluation and reliability, including the limits of LLM-as-judge protocols
  • Scalable infrastructure for training and serving

Agents and embodied intelligence

Forward-looking interests, not completed work. I have not published in these areas; I am listing them because they are where I would like to take the representation-level questions above.

  • Agentic systems that plan and act over long horizons
  • Multimodal grounding
  • Embodied settings, where a scenario is something a system perceives and acts within rather than only reads
